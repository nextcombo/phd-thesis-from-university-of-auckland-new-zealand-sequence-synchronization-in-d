# PhD Thesis from University of Auckland, New Zealand - Sequence Synchronization in Direct-Sequence Spread Spectrum Communication Systems Based on Chaos [with MATLAB Code]

This paper investigates the modeling and performance of sequence synchronization in direct sequence spread spectrum (DS-SS) communication systems based on chaos, with the aim of identifying gaps in existing research and proposing a new, precise method. The DS-SS system provides physical layer security without significantly increasing computational or power consumption, which is significant in scenarios where traditional encryption techniques are unable to be applied due to high power consumption and computational intensiveness.
The core focus of the paper is on the problem of synchronization of chaos spread spectrum sequences. Previous modeling methods for chaos spread spectrum codes have not considered their non-binary characteristics, leading to inaccuracies. This paper expands the analysis of existing literature by incorporating DS-SS synchronization into channel fading scenarios, thereby proposing a precise method for modeling the DS-SS system's synchronization module. This method, based on the statistical description of the cross-correlation and auto-correlation functions of the chaos spread spectrum code, is called Chaos Correlation Statistics (CCS). To validate the effectiveness of this method, the paper models the capture phase of the DS-SS synchronization module in three scenarios (noise, fading, and interleaving), and results show that the CCS method can accurately predict the performance of these three scenarios during the capture phase. Additionally, the CCS method has been used to statistically model the tracking loop in DS-SS, and its accuracy has been verified through comparison with numerical analysis results. The paper also derives the impact of tracking loop error and timing jitter on bit error probability under the above three scenarios, and the newly derived bit error probability equation has been verified through extensive simulation results.
Overall, the research conclusions indicate that the sequence synchronization in DS-SS systems is feasible, and the CCS method is an appropriate tool for accurately predicting the performance of DS-SS system synchronization. Moreover, the CCS method can be applied to any scenario requiring precise modeling of the correlation function of non-binary spread spectrum sequences.
Introduction "Mr. Camp, can you hear anything?" On December 12, 1901, Guglielmo Marconi spoke without emotion. Mr. Camp picked up the receiver and after a moment, clearly audible three short beeps - the point signal of the letter "S" was transmitted from Cornwall in England to Signal Hill in Newfoundland [1]. This marked the first transatlantic wireless transmission. The ability to transmit wireless signals over 3000 kilometers was significant, and Marconi hesitated for two days before announcing this news, fearing it might be too incredible to be believed [1]. This was a significant milestone in the development of wireless communication.
In the following years, the user base for wireless signals (all in the form of radio telegraphy) continued to expand. The navy was the main purchaser of wireless equipment for shore-to-ship communication [2, pp-69]. In the 1910s, all intercontinental transportation relied on sea transport, with radio telegraphy being primarily used by the shipping industry [2, pp-69]. In 1912, a distress signal sent by Marconi's operator on the Titanic was received by nearby ships, ultimately saving 700 lives. The British Postmaster General summarized: "Those who were saved owe their lives to Mr. Marconi and his great invention."
Like many other technological fields, communication was largely driven by military needs. During World War I, both sides used both wired and wireless signal technologies [3, pp406]. Due to the vulnerability of cables and the possibility of enemy eavesdropping, the British army adopted wireless technology during the war. The Germans used wireless signals to guide their bombers [4], and both sides also used them for artillery aiming correction and air support [3, pp-407]; interestingly, post-war reports indicated that the French successfully deceived the Germans by sending false wireless signals [5]. These were early examples of how information transmission security became important in military scenarios.
During World War II, the reliance on telecommunications grew, and the requirements for transmission security became stricter. Both the Axis and Allied powers developed new methods to transmit coordinates and military messages using radio waves, but due to similar technological foundations, systems were relatively similar. The most notable technology widely used during World War II was spread spectrum (SS) communication. Hattie Lamar and George Ansel had the first public patent for SS in military applications, which was approved in 1942 but details remained classified as military secrets [6, pp-3]. The inventors handed it over to the U.S. government for use during the war until the patent expired before entering the commercial domain [6, pp-3]. SS technology was used during World War II to send secret messages and guide torpedoes, but details remain largely undisclosed [6, pp-4]. From 1948 to 1968, the commercial application of wireless communication was driven by the development of frequency modulation (FM) technology, which compared to AM used before World War II was an improvement [2, pp-35].
The application of wireless systems is not limited to cellular systems but also includes other forms of wireless networks, such as wireless local area networks (WLANs) following Wi-Fi standards and mobile broadband internet access via WiMAX protocols. Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are among the emerging technologies that have benefited greatly from recent advances in wireless communications. These low-cost and easily deployed sensors can be placed anywhere and relay collected data to storage/processing units through each other. WSNs have various applications in both military and civilian fields, and their number continues to increase with the enhancement of network robustness.
Among the various wireless communication methods developed, spread spectrum technology remains important for its anti-interference and security capabilities. However, today's secure spread spectrum systems require a new method of spreading for physical layer security due to the powerful computing capabilities of transmitters and receivers. In low-power scenarios, this demand has led to the development of chaos communication, one branch of which is applied to spread spectrum systems.
Almost all existing digital communication schemes require a certain degree of timing synchronization between transmitters and receivers, including spread spectrum systems. The process of acquiring and maintaining this timing alignment is known as synchronization. For chaotic spread spectrum systems to be effective communication tools, it is also necessary to achieve precise and secure timing estimation between the transmitter and receiver. The characteristics of chaotic spread spectrum signals make synchronization a non-trivial problem.
The primary focus of this paper is to explore the current level of technology in synchronization for chaotic spread spectrum systems, identifying its shortcomings. Building on this, the paper aims to propose a new method to model the synchronization process of chaotic spread spectrum systems, aiming to overcome these shortcomings. This method is tested through different communication channel scenarios and validated through extensive simulations. Finally, the paper theoretically analyzes the impact of imperfect synchronization on system performance, proposing mathematical expressions for evaluating the performance of chaotic spread spectrum systems. Overall, by introducing a new analytical approach, this paper significantly expands the research findings of existing literature, filling the gap in the accurate modeling of the synchronization module for chaotic spread spectrum systems.
Conclusion
Since Marconi's first wireless signal transmission across the Atlantic on December 12, 1901, there has been a continuous demand for robust wireless communication methods, serving as a driving force for research, invention, and innovation. Subsequently, various wireless communication schemes were proposed and applied. As dependence on wireless communication increases, the sensitivity of information transmitted via wireless waves also grows. The issue of transmitting sensitive information using wireless technology highlights the need for secure wireless communication methods. Moreover, in scenarios involving secure information transmission, it is necessary to guard against malicious parties disrupting communications through interference. Therefore, there is a need for robust and secure wireless communication solutions.
Despite the traditional use of encryption and spread spectrum communication for securing wireless communication systems, in many scenarios, encryption is not the best choice due to computational or energy limitations. Moreover, having an additional layer of security is beneficial, as in many cases, people not only want to hide data but even want to conceal the fact that wireless communication is taking place. The use of chaotic signals addresses these scenarios. Chaotic signals are characterized by their quasi-randomness, broadband characteristics, and ease of generation, proving to be ideal choices for spreading sequences in spread spectrum communication (including direct sequence spread spectrum systems).
To successfully design and operate a chaos-based direct sequence spread spectrum (CDS-SS) system, there must be a robust method for synchronizing chaos spreading sequences. Since the precise synchronization of chaos sequences directly affects system performance, ensuring robust synchronization of chaos sequences is key to the practicality of CDS-SS systems. For this purpose, it is essential to understand the accurate behavior of chaos sequences under synchronization (capturing and tracking) contexts.
This paper investigates the problem of capturing and tracking chaos sequences in spread spectrum scenarios, using transmit pilots to obtain precise timing between the transmitter and receiver. The objective of the paper is firstly to study the current level of technology in CDS-SS synchronization, identifying existing shortcomings. Research has shown that previous analyses of CDS-SS synchronization were not accurate because they did not consider channel fading issues; more fundamentally, existing literature on CDS-SS related papers failed to derive accurate synchronization performance expressions because their system analysis methods directly borrowed from traditional spread spectrum code systems.
Therefore, the second objective of the paper is to find a more accurate method for analyzing CDS-SS systems to take into account the unique properties of chaos sequences. Research indicates that the analysis method based on chaos correlation function statistical modeling is a suitable way to model the synchronization module of the CDS-SS system, which is called chaos correlation statistical (CCS).
The paper models the correlation of chaotic signals as two Gaussian random variables: one with a mean of zero, corresponding to the correlation function distribution when the local generated sequence is not aligned with the transmitted pilots; and another with a non-zero mean, corresponding to the autocorrelation peak when the two sequences are perfectly aligned. The variance of these two parts of the correlation function was derived analytically and verified through simulations. Next, the impact of additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) and square law devices on correlation will be incorporated into the Gaussian model.

In the second channel scenario, the effect of slow flat fading is incorporated into the Gaussian model, and the results indicate that when there is a slow frequency flat fading, the CCS model can accurately predict the performance during the capture phase. The simulation receiver operating characteristic (ROC) results are highly consistent with theoretical predictions. The decline in capture phase performance under fading conditions prompts researchers to adopt code interleaving techniques to mitigate the effects of fading. Therefore, in the third channel scenario, blind block code interleaving / de-interleaving is included in the capture phase. The statistical model based on the CCS method is highly consistent with the simulation ROC results, indicating that it is now possible to accurately predict capture performance across various channel scenarios.
As part of the second objective of the research, this study also investigated the tracking phase of the CDS-SS synchronous module. The primary goal was to statistically model the peak of the tracking loop S curve (error curve). Since the tracking loop employs a delay lock structure (using two correlated elements with certain delays), the CCS method can be applied. The results indicate that the CCS method can accurately model the statistical characteristics of the tracking loop S curve. The distribution of the S curve peak is derived through theoretical deduction and verified through simulation results. This finding is significant because existing literature on CDS-SS tracking typically assumes the tracking S curve is constant, whereas this paper shows it varies depending on the sequence, making the statistical methods used for modeling the S curve more suitable and accurate in this paper.
After modeling the tracking phase of the CDS-SS system synchronous module, it was found that minor tracking errors were caused by random timing variations (termed timing jitter). Therefore, the paper focused on investigating the impact of tracking errors on overall system performance. Firstly, the energy loss due to partial code chip overlap was calculated and associated with the misalignment quantity, which was then used to re-derive the error probability equation for partially overlapping code chips. By comparing the theoretical bit error rate (BER) curves with simulation results, the effectiveness of this method was validated. After establishing its effectiveness in an AWGN scenario, the study was extended to systems containing fading channels as well as those using code chip interleaving / de-interleaving, with accuracy verified through simulations in both scenarios. Finally, assuming that the level of timing jitter is random and follows a uniform distribution or a known variance Gaussian distribution, the results showed that although the error probability equation obtained does not have a closed-form solution, it can accurately predict system performance, which is confirmed by a high degree of consistency with simulation results.
Overall, this paper confirms the feasibility of synchronizing chaos-spread spectrum sequences in the CDS-SS system. Furthermore, the paper proposes a method suitable for modeling the correlation function of chaos-spread spectrum sequences. This method has been tested and proven to accurately predict the performance of synchronization modules during capture and tracking stages under different channel scenarios. This method can be applied to future research on chaos communication extensions that rely on solving the correlation function of chaos sequences.
